Insights from the APAN58 REN Leaders Forum: Advancing NREN Capabilities with GenAI.

The 58th Asia Pacific Advanced Network (APAN58) Meeting was held in Islamabad, Pakistan, from the 26th to the 30th of August 2024. Organised by the Pakistan Education & Research Network (PERN) and the Higher Education Commission of Pakistan, the event welcomed delegates and guests from around the world for four days of meaningful discussions that would promote collaboration and contribute to sustainable societal well-being. This event marked the first time Pakistan hosted a physical meeting of APAN. In today's digital world, APAN serves as a vital platform for researchers and technical experts from across the region to share insights and forge valuable partnerships.

APAN58 also featured a Special Interest Group (SIG), which facilitated a forum for REN leaders to exchange ideas and thoughts on the present trends, challenges, and opportunities faced by the REN communities. This 90-minute forum comprised a closed assembly of C-Level executives and decision-makers from RENs across the Asia-Pacific region, designed to facilitate high-level strategic deliberations among the leaders of National Research and Education Networks (NRENs) in the APAC region — serving as a platform to collectively address challenges and opportunities to empower researchers and academics across borders.

Capacity Building: Generative AI for NRENs

An overview of the AP-GAINED project. One of the key focuses of the REN Leaders Forum was understanding how NRENs are utilising Generative AI (GenAI) to enhance their services and capabilities. Opening the discussion, the CEO of LEARN, Prof. Roshan Ragel, reflected on the discussions from the previous APAN57 meeting in Bangkok, where it was identified that there were no established training programs focused on GenAI. In response to this gap, LEARN has developed a proposal in collaboration with BdREN to address this issue through the AP-GAINED project.

This AP-GAINED initiative aims to build capacity by training NREN staff on the effective use of GenAI technologies. The project's first step involves identifying stakeholders' needs to develop a tailored curriculum. Already, the exploration has revealed that GenAI holds immense potential in sectors such as finance, education, healthcare, and agriculture, among others. During the discussion, the Chief Administrative and Finance Officer at BdREN, Dr. Mohammed Farooq Ali Tarafder, emphasised the importance of training non-technical staff at NRENs, highlighting the growing need for these staff members to be proficient in utilising cutting-edge technologies like GenAI to ensure smooth operations and broader impact across the sectors they support.

Key Findings: GenAI for NREN Empowerment Survey

Subsequently, the CTO of LEARN, Dr. Asitha Bandaranayake, presented the results of a survey titled "GenAI for NREN Empowerment," conducted before the session among NREN personnel. The survey aimed to quantify the needs and challenges faced by the community across academic, technical, and administrative job roles, with a particular focus on the potential of GenAI in enhancing their workflows. While the majority of participants were from Pakistan, there was diverse representation from other regions, across roles including engineers, administrative staff, management, academic staff, and research personnel — most having worked at their respective NRENs for over a year.

One of the survey's primary objectives was to assess the daily challenges faced by NREN staff. It was found that most of their tasks required communication skills, followed closely by technical and administrative skills; a significant challenge was the limited support for research activities. The survey also found that most participants had engaged with ChatGPT and found it highly efficient for tasks such as report writing, proposal development, and creating educational content — though users reported inefficiencies in daily or weekly use, suggesting room for improvement.

Notably, results showed NREN staff are open to adopting new tools, with many identifying time savings as the key success metric. Most rated their organisations as moderately ready to integrate GenAI and were confident they would benefit from its adoption; however, cost emerged as the primary barrier. In terms of training, hands-on, in-person sessions were the most requested format, preferred over online options. Dr. Bandaranayake called for a combination of physical and online learning opportunities to help NREN staff develop these skills. Detailed survey results are included in the appendix below.

Addressing Common Challenges Faced by NRENs

The REN Leaders Forum at APAN58 addressed several critical challenges and opportunities in implementing GenAI across NRENs in the Asia-Pacific region: the high cost of GenAI tools for academics, the need for Large Language Models (LLMs) that support local languages, lessons from Japan's comprehensive approach to AI policy-making, and the importance of accurate data and strategic leadership in guiding automation efforts.

Prohibitive costs of GenAI tools for academics. Prof. Ragel emphasised that the prohibitive cost of GenAI tools was a serious issue, confirmed by the survey findings, sharing that several member institutions of LEARN had already requested assistance in reducing these costs. He proposed that regional NRENs, including LEARN, could explore negotiating with GenAI service providers to establish a subsidised education subscription tier specifically for NREN member institutes.

The Growing Need for Large Language Models That Support Local Languages

Prof. Ragel mentioned that the community has expressed a need for LLMs that support local languages in Asia, though providing the necessary training infrastructure remains a complex issue. President of ThaiREN, Dr. Chalermpol Charnsripinyo, echoed this need, pointing out that such initiatives require significant High-Performance Computing (HPC) resources, and suggested that NRENs could share resources and datasets — for example, for training AI models for medical imaging. ThaiREN already offers a publicly available AI service platform used by researchers and businesses for trials, demonstrating how shared infrastructure can enable innovation.

Prof. Ragel supported this idea, noting that the APAN Open Data Group is already working on making such datasets available, and that NRENs could play a role by providing storage solutions. He also noted that while technical tasks might be sufficiently handled by English-based GenAI models, non-technical tasks often require far more local-language support — suggesting this be examined further at a later stage.

Lessons From Japan's Approach to GenAI

Chairman of APAN, Prof. Shinji Shimojo, explained that Japan's focus on AI has been broad, encompassing not just the work of specific NRENs but also involving academia in shaping national AI policies. Universities in Japan have been actively involved, utilising AI for creating educational content and streamlining administrative tasks. Prof. Shimojo also noted that the Japanese government is already exploring significant investments in large-scale computing facilities aimed at developing LLMs that support the Japanese language.

Ensuring the Effectiveness of GenAI

Towards the latter half of the discussion, the CEO of BdREN, Mohammad Tawrit, highlighted a critical factor in ensuring the effectiveness of GenAI: the accuracy of the data — without which AI tools may struggle to provide correct insights or solutions. Dr. Bandaranayake added that AI applications can be viewed from two perspectives: generating valuable insights to assist strategic decisions, and automating repetitive tasks to free up working professionals for more productive activities. However, he stressed that all applications of AI must align with the overarching vision set by leadership, who must ensure automation efforts stay aligned with the NREN's long-term strategic direction.

Unlocking the Potential Applications of GenAI

The APAN58 REN Leaders Forum highlighted the transformative potential of GenAI for NRENs in the Asia-Pacific region, underscoring capacity building as a key driver of successful adoption. Collaborative efforts such as the AP-GAINED project by LEARN and BdREN are poised to address the need for targeted training programmes that equip NREN personnel with the skills to harness GenAI capabilities. The survey results reinforced this by revealing that staff are eager to adopt new tools and bridge the knowledge gap between technical and non-technical roles. While challenges such as cost and local-language support remain pressing, the discussions also spotlighted the potential of GenAI in improving workflows, enhancing decision-making, and automating repetitive tasks. By fostering collaboration among regional NRENs, there is a significant opportunity to pool resources and knowledge, ultimately improving academic and research communities across the region.
